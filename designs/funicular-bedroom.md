# LAST STOP — funicular bedroom

A standalone object design brief for a decommissioned funicular car converted into a personal bedroom. The character is **neon, punk and cozy**: an old machine that someone has repaired, adapted and made their own.

This is a proposed design, ready for blockout. The existing station renders have not been changed by this brief. Dimensions are working targets to verify in the standalone model.

## The central idea

From outside, it should still read immediately as a funicular: curved roof, large front windscreens, twin round headlights, cream window frames, teal bodywork and exposed undercarriage. Through the glass, it should read as someone's home. A warm reading lamp, rumpled bedding and handmade curtains establish that transformation before the viewer notices individual props.

The punk character comes from visible decisions: bolted repairs, mismatched fittings, patched fabric, a hand-painted sign, stickers accumulated around the entrance and old controls given new jobs. The neon is a small, deliberate addition to that history. The room should feel inhabited and cared for, with a few unfinished repairs.

## Layout and scale

Use the current approximately **2.4 × 5.1 m exterior footprint** as the starting point. Allow roughly **2.15–2.25 m finished interior width** after lining. Keep the car stationary on a short section of track; a leveled interior floor insert accommodates the parked chassis. Confirm approximately 1.95–2.05 m standing headroom in the central circulation zone and comfortable seated clearance over the mattress before detailing the shell.

Define the front as the end with the paired windscreens and headlights.

| Zone, front to rear | Arrangement | Important visual cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Former driver's cab | A 45–55 cm deep timber desk beneath the windscreen, with the original control panel retained along one edge. A small stool tucks completely underneath. | The desk follows the curved front rather than resembling a freestanding office desk. |
| Middle / entrance | Retain the side entry, with a narrow landing, coat hooks, boot tray and a small fold-down shelf. Leave a clear place to stand and turn. | Open door, thick doorway reveal and a view through the car establish real interior depth. |
| Rear-left sleeping zone | A 120 × 190 cm lengthwise mattress on a low platform, with its head against the rear bulkhead. Lift-up access reaches storage below. | Soft bedding contrasts with the hard metal shell; the bed is visible through the side windows. |
| Rear-right aisle / storage | Aim for a clear 65–75 cm route beside the bed, with shallow 25–30 cm storage where the finished width permits. | Continuous visible floor connects the entrance, desk and bed. |

These zones share space: the desk stool uses the aisle while occupied, and the bedding must stay inside the mattress envelope. Check that the original door mechanism has clearance. Use a physically consistent retained sliding mechanism or a deliberately retrofitted hinged door in the blockout.

## Exterior: preserve the vehicle, show the conversion

Keep the barrel roof and original window rhythm as the strongest shapes. Add convincing thickness to the roof edge, window seals, door jambs and lower sill. Rounded corners should have manufactured radii and straight spans between them.

The lower body is faded petroleum teal. Warm ivory trim is yellowed and chipped around fasteners. The roof is dull zinc with darker seams and a repaired patch near a small ventilation cowl. Concentrate rust around seams, drains, lower corners and damaged paint; let broad sheltered panels remain relatively quiet.

Give the entrance one memorable asymmetrical repair: a dark steel patch plate with exposed bolts and a hand-painted identification stencil. Retain a few transit markings and the large **02**. A small destination box reads **LAST STOP**; a secondary handwritten plate beside the door reads **HOME**. Typography should be legible at the intended camera distance.

Use a compact magenta illuminated sign near the entrance, mounted on a believable bracket with a cable disappearing into a service box. A faint cyan courtesy light sits under the entry step. Restrict stickers and old gig flyers to the door surround and one interior panel, with overlaps, faded edges and a few torn remnants.

## Interior: domestic layers inside an industrial shell

Expose parts of the original curved metal ribs overhead. Fit warm plywood lining between selected ribs, retaining cream-painted metal around the windows. A slim timber strip at hand height, a worn rug in the entrance and a softer floor finish make the conversion feel comfortable at the points people touch.

Build the bed as an integrated plywood platform with rounded exposed corners and a recessed toe space. Its quilt is faded burgundy or oxblood, repaired with several visibly different fabric patches. Add an oatmeal sheet, a slightly compressed pillow, one mustard cushion and a loosely folded wool blanket. Give the textiles weight: broad folds, contact compression and a little sag between supports.

Hang muted olive or charcoal curtains from the former luggage rail. One panel is tied back with a repurposed strap. Leave a glazed bay clear so the exterior view can reveal the bed. Fit a shallow bedside niche into the lining for a mug, a paperback and a warm reading light.

At the front, keep the worn control lever, a few switches and an old gauge. Add a fitted timber desk surface around them, a small battered audio player and a notebook. A clearly labeled pair of original switches now controls **ROOM** and **SIGN** lighting. Tuck cables into clips along ribs and let one service loop remain visible.

## Lighting hierarchy

The room's dominant light is warm, approximately **2400–2700 K**: a shaded bedside lamp plus soft light concealed beneath the former luggage rail. Keep some ceiling bays and corners in shadow so the interior has depth.

Magenta comes mainly from the small entrance sign, catching the door frame, nearby metal and one curtain edge. Cyan is weaker and local to the step or a short cable channel near the desk. The bed retains warm, readable fabric colors. Cool blue light from the windows supplies the connection to the outside.

Every bright accent needs a visible fixture, or a clear concealed mounting position. Judge neon brightness by what it illuminates and reflects; keep lettering legible rather than turning it into a white bloom.

## Materials and wear

| Material | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Teal painted steel | Broad faded paint, fine scratches, localized exposed primer and rust at vulnerable edges. |
| Ivory enamel / rubber seals | Slight yellowing, compressed dark gaskets, grime tucked into joints. |
| Plywood / timber | Visible layered cut edges, restrained grain, rounded worn contact points. |
| Glass | Proper thickness, rubber channels and limited fingerprints near handles; preserve useful views inside. |
| Bedding / curtains | Coarse fabric, stitched patches, uneven folds and compressed contact areas. |
| Exposed repair hardware | Blackened steel brackets, a few mismatched bolts, oxidized brass on handled fittings. |

Avoid applying one noise/bump pattern to every material. Model larger dents, hems, weld seams and protruding fittings where their silhouette matters. Use surface shading for fine grain and restrained micro-wear.

## Signature details

1. **The destination box:** LAST STOP above a vehicle that has become a permanent home.
2. **The converted cab:** original controls surrounding a fitted timber desk.
3. **The patched quilt:** the main soft, human shape inside the industrial shell.
4. **The entrance repair:** bolted plate, handmade HOME label, clustered stickers and restrained colored light.
5. **The reused luggage rail:** curtain support, reading-light mount and traces of its former transit function.

Keep a clear visual hierarchy: car silhouette first; warm bedroom second; personal details third.

## How to model and evaluate it

Build it as a separate asset in local coordinates, with shell, undercarriage, glazing, interior, textiles, lighting and props in separate collections. Keep the roof removable for inspection while providing an intact exterior view. Retain believable wall thickness and enough space to move a camera through the doorway.

Start with the body, leveled floor, mattress, entry and desk at actual scale. Test three views before spending time on small details:

- A low three-quarter exterior view showing the round front, side entry and warm bed through a clear window.
- A view through the open side door showing the bed, aisle and cab at different depths.
- A roof-off layout view for checking fit and modeling; present it explicitly as a cutaway.

Use shaped profiles and bevels for the body, curves for seals and cables, and shared meshes for repeated fittings. Use cloth simulation or controlled sculpted folds for the bedding after the mattress and support surfaces are fixed. Procedural tools are useful for rivets, seams and restrained variation. External AI-generated meshes may contribute individual props later; the body, layout and main furniture need deliberate geometry and dimensions.

The blockout succeeds when the car remains recognizable, the bed and entry actually fit, the front-to-back depth is readable, and the warm interior remains inviting with the neon switched off. Subsequent passes can add textiles, lighting and localized wear without losing that structure.
